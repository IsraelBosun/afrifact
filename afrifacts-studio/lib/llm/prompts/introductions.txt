## What counts as an introduction

An introduction says who the person is in the world, independent of
the event the sentence describes: their role, job, title or claim to
fame.

- Counts: "the comedian known for playing Lawyer Kunle", "Nigeria's
  first president", "the industrialist who founded the Ibru
  Organisation", "Queen Victoria's goddaughter", "a British naval
  captain", "King Ghezo of Dahomey", "President Buhari".
- It can be very short. One or two words are enough: "footballer Samuel
  Okwaraji", "activist Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti", "chef Hilda Baci".
- A title that says what they were counts: King, Queen, President,
  Governor, Emir, Oba, General, Captain, Prime Minister.
- A link to someone FAMOUS counts, because the fame carries it:
  "Dangote's great-grandfather", "Fela's mother", "the son of Nigeria's
  finance minister". Report it as kind "role".
- An honorific alone does NOT count: Chief, Dr, Mr, Mrs, Alhaji, Sir.
  "Chief Michael Ibru" is not introduced.
- What happens to them IN THIS SENTENCE does not count. "Sarah Forbes
  Bonetta, orphaned in 1848 and marked for sacrifice" tells you the
  event, not why anyone knows her name. Kind "none".
- Being the relative of someone NOT famous does not count: "the daughter
  of a trader", "his uncle Musa". Kind "relation".
- A job that is not why they are known does not count: calling a famous
  footballer "a qualified lawyer" does not tell a stranger he is a
  footballer. Kind "none".
- Use only the words in the sentence, never what you know yourself.

One person under two names ("Sarah Forbes Bonetta, born Aina") is ONE
person: list them once.

Only NAMED people. Leave out:
- places, buildings and things named after people ("Port Harcourt",
  "the Great Pyramid of Cheops"): the name there is a place, not a
  person the reader has to know;
- unnamed people ("a woman", "his mother", "a census officer");
- companies, countries and peoples.
