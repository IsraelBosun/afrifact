## What has been measured about where strong facts come from

- The DOCUMENT decides almost everything. A rich document yields 4 to 7
  strong facts; a thin one yields none however it is read.
- Rich: long articles about a PERSON with a dramatic life, a scandal or
  crime, a dynasty with palace intrigue, a famous family, a person who
  links two worlds (someone from {{country}} behind a global product, a celebrity with
  an unexpected parent), an institution with a strange history.
- Thin: articles that are mostly administration, geography, lists, or
  scholarly debate (for example a kingdom article about its ritual
  offices gave 0 strong facts from 15). Short stubs are thin by
  definition: prefer articles over about 2,000 words.
- Readers want surprise: a hidden link, a number that reframes something
  familiar, a person doing something startling. They reject what every
  schoolchild knows and what is merely obscure.

## Variety is a rule, not a preference

The run keeps ONE fact per document and ONE fact per subject, and at
most {{perCategory}} per category. A document about someone already
covered in this run will not be read, and facts that name a covered
subject are set aside. So after every read, move to an UNRELATED
subject: a different person, a different field, ideally a different
category. Never follow a family, a company or a story to its neighbours.

## What to do next

Choose exactly ONE action.

- search: look for documents. Wikipedia search matches article titles
  and names, not descriptions: "Hilda Baci" or "Nigerian Railway
  Corporation" finds an article, "Nigerian con artist international
  biography" finds nothing useful. So think of a SPECIFIC name first,
  then search for it. Two or three words. Set "web" to true only
  when Wikipedia is unlikely to have a good article (a recent event, a
  business story, a living person's lesser-known work); a web search
  spends a scarce paid budget.
- read: read one search result by its id (r1, r2, ...). Pick the one
  most likely to be rich, by the rules above.
- finish: stop, when the target is met or when you judge further
  searching is not worth the budget.

Think about what the last observation tells you. If a document yielded
nothing strong, do not read its near-twin next. If a document yielded a
strong fact, that subject is done: go somewhere unrelated.
